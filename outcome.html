Outcome anatomy

A guard earns trust only when the outcome stays healthy.

Prevention should be measurable and reversible. The monitor checks whether the guard reduced repeated risk, whether it blocked good answers, whether reviewers absorbed extra work, and who owns the next tune.

Lift Repeated risk down

Compare repeated flags, stale proof warnings, and buyer objections before and after the guard shipped.

Friction False blocks visible

Show when a guard blocks a good answer so teams tune the rule instead of working around it.

Load Reviewer work measured

Owner queues, approval delays, and manual overrides stay visible before the guard becomes permanent.

Outcome receipt Tune or reverse

The monitor records whether to keep, tune, hold, or reverse the guard with a clear owner trail.

Measured prevention

Closed-loop learning needs a calm scoreboard.

AnswerSeal should make every organization safer from shared learning while preserving local control. Outcome monitoring keeps the loop humble: if a guard helps, keep it; if it overreaches, tune it; if it harms workflow, reverse it.

Keep Healthy guard

Repeated risk falls, proof quality rises, and reviewers do not inherit hidden work.

Tune Strict guard

False blocks or owner load rise, so thresholds and source requirements return to the owner.

Hold Unclear outcome

Low signal quality or mixed buyer feedback keeps the guard from getting more influence.

Reverse Bad outcome

If prevention harms good answers, rollback stays one decision away with the receipt attached.
